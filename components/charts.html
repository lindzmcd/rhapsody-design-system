Charts, graphs, and tables

Data visuals follow the same rules as everything else: brand colors, generous space, and no decoration. Charts are the one place the three-color rule relaxes, use as many brand colors as the data needs to stay clear, but no more than necessary. Lead with the point. Titles in navy, gridlines light gray, labels in dark gray. Avoid gradients, 3D effects, and heavy borders.

Charts and graphs

Grouped bar: blue leads, navy for the comparison series, light gridlines, navy title.

Line: blue, teal, purple, and orange series, direct dots, minimal legend, no clutter.

Stacked bar: the full sequence (blue, teal, light blue, purple), only as many as the data needs.

Donut: blue leads with teal, light blue, purple, and orange; gray for the remainder; key number in the center.

  • Use Rhapsody colors, adding more only when the data needs distinguishing. Blue leads, then navy, teal, light blue, purple, and orange; gray for remainder or context. Blue and navy make a strong two-series pairing. This data-series order is for distinguishing values and is separate from the build/automate wayfinding order (see Pathways).
  • Navy titles, dark gray axis labels, light gray gridlines. Remove top and right borders.
  • No gradients, 3D, drop shadows, or heavy outlines. Data first.
  • Label directly where possible; keep legends minimal.
