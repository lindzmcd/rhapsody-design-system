Charts, graphs, and tables

Data visuals follow the same rules as everything else: brand colors, generous space, and no decoration. Charts are the one place the three-color rule relaxes, use as many brand colors as the data needs to stay clear, but no more than necessary. Lead with the point. Titles in navy, gridlines light gray, labels in dark gray. Avoid gradients, 3D effects, and heavy borders.

Charts and graphs

Grouped bar: blue leads, navy for the comparison series, light gridlines, navy title.

Line: series in the chart color order (blue, navy, teal, purple), direct dots, minimal legend. Light blue is skipped here, it is too faint as a thin line on white.

Stacked bar: the sequence in order (blue, navy, teal, light blue), only as many as the data needs.

Donut: segments in order (blue, navy, teal, light blue, purple); gray for the remainder; key number in the center.

Chart color order

When a chart needs more than one color, assign them in this fixed order so every chart reads as one system. Add only as many as the data needs, and use gray for a remainder or context series. This data-series order is for distinguishing values and is separate from the Pathways build/automate wayfinding order.

1 · Blue
2 · Navy
3 · Teal
4 · Light blue
5 · Purple
6 · Orange
Gray · remainder

Color rules in charts

  • Blue leads, then navy; blue and navy make a strong two-series pairing. Blue, navy, and teal are the workhorse series colors.
  • Light blue is for fills and large areas (bar, stacked, and donut segments), not thin lines or small marks on white, where it is too low-contrast. On navy it reads at any size.
  • Orange is a sparing accent, a few marks at most, never a whole palette of orange. Put dark (navy or black) labels on orange fills.
  • Purple rounds out the sequence; don’t set purple as labels on navy or black.
  • Teal fills take dark labels only (navy or black), never white.
  • Gray (#D9DFE6) is the remainder or context series, and the gridline color. Titles navy, axis labels dark gray.
  • Stay within the order and add a color only when the data needs distinguishing; charts are the one place the three-color rule relaxes.

Chart style

  • No gradients, 3D, drop shadows, or heavy outlines. Data first.
  • Label directly where possible; keep legends minimal. Remove top and right borders.
