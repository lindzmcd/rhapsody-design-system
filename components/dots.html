Dots overview

The dots are the primary graphic device for Rhapsody, and they carry the meaning of what we do. Each dot is a data point, a system, a person. Rhapsody works to connect that data, clean it, and organize it, and the dots tell that story: many separate points brought into alignment, resolving from scattered noise into an ordered, legible field. Used well, they signal connectivity, precision, and scale while adding texture and refinement.

How the dots work

Pick a background, a color mode, and the composition. The main composition is the uniform scattered field; at small sizes the big dot (one oversized dot resolving into a few) carries the same story. Each device has its own card (Scattered dot field, Big dot); the smaller decorative uses live in Dot accents. Whatever you choose, the dots stay to a corner or edge, cover no more than 50 percent of the space, and never overlap text.

Dot field or big dot?

Device What it says When to use
Scattered dot field Scale and order: many equal points resolving from noise into one aligned, legible field. The precise, infrastructure register. Larger canvases with room to breathe, full or section backgrounds, web heroes, slide and report covers, booth graphics, where the field can dissolve gradually from its edge.
Big dot Relationship and momentum: systems of every size coming into connection, with Rhapsody as the anchor. The warmer, more human register. Small, fast-read formats where a full field is too busy or too small to resolve, display ads, social tiles, thin banners, cover slides, plus hero and brand moments that want warmth.

Use one device per layout, never both, and never a dot device together with a light device. The decorative dot accents (Dot accents) are a separate, lighter use.

Backgrounds and dot colors

Each field and big dot is built for one background and is not interchangeable. The background sets which dot colors are allowed:

Background Dot colors
Navy #0B2C47 White, Rhapsody blue, light blue, teal, purple, plus a maximum of 3 orange.
Rhapsody blue #1A81F4 White only.
White #FFFFFF Navy, Rhapsody blue, light blue, teal, purple, plus a maximum of 3 orange.

Orange lives in multicolor only, never on a Rhapsody blue ground, and never more than 3 dots so it reads as a surprise. The dots are exempt from the three-color rule. Full detail for each device: Scattered dot field and Big dot.
