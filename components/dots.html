Dot patterns

The dots pattern

The dots are the primary graphic device for Rhapsody, and they carry the meaning of what we do. Each dot is a data point, a system, a person. Rhapsody works to connect that data, clean it, and organize it, and the dots tell that story: many separate points brought into alignment, resolving from scattered noise into an ordered, legible field. Used well, they signal connectivity, precision, and scale while adding texture and refinement.

How the dots work

Pick a background, a color mode, and the composition. The main composition is the uniform scattered field; at small sizes the big dot (one oversized dot resolving into a few) carries the same story; and dots can also be composed into framing and diagram elements. All are covered below. Whatever you choose, the dots stay to a corner or edge, cover no more than 50 percent of the space, and never overlap text.

Dot field or big dot?

Device What it says When to use
Scattered dot field Scale and order: many equal points resolving from noise into one aligned, legible field. The precise, infrastructure register. Larger canvases with room to breathe, full or section backgrounds, web heroes, slide and report covers, booth graphics, where the field can dissolve gradually from its edge.
Big dot Relationship and momentum: systems of every size coming into connection, with Rhapsody as the anchor. The warmer, more human register. Small, fast-read formats where a full field is too busy or too small to resolve, display ads, social tiles, thin banners, cover slides, plus hero and brand moments that want warmth.

Either device, never both in one layout, and the framing and diagram elements below are a third, more decorative use.

Backgrounds and dot colors

Each field is built for one background and is not interchangeable. The background sets which dot colors are allowed:

Background Dot colors
Navy #0B2C47 White, Rhapsody blue, light blue, teal, purple, plus a maximum of 3 orange.
Rhapsody blue #1A81F4 White only.
White #FFFFFF Navy, Rhapsody blue, light blue, teal, purple, plus a maximum of 3 orange.

Orange lives in multicolor fields only, never on a Rhapsody blue ground, and never more than 3 dots so it reads as a surprise. The field is exempt from the three-color rule.

The dot field

The dot field is structured dispersion: a cohesive cluster of identical dots on one invisible grid that gathers into a dense core at an edge or corner and dissolves unevenly outward into a transition zone and a few connected outliers. Energetic and fluid, but intelligently organized, randomized, never random. It never forms a recognizable object, symbol, letter, line, wave, or geometric shape. (The older graduated field, where dot size changed across the grid, is retired.)

A closed, approved family, use it, don’t build it

The masters are a closed, approved set, one for each background, anchor, and format, each as a transparent SVG and a 2× PNG. Pick the file that fits and use it as-is, flipped, or rotated 90°. Do not hand-place a new field, and never restyle one, no recoloring, resizing individual dots, gradients, opacity, strokes, or non-proportional stretching. Hand-built fields drift off the grid and off-brand.

The three anchors

The anchor is where the field’s centre of gravity sits. Keep all copy in the clear area opposite it.

Bottom, core on the lower edge, biased right. Copy in the top half.

Right, core on the right edge, biased to the vertical centre. Copy on the left half.

Corner, core in the bottom-right corner. Copy top-left.

Formats

Five formats cover the common canvases: 16×9 (slides, web hero, video), 1×1 (social), 4×5 (social portrait), 9×16 (story, vertical video), and a 2560×720 banner (email header, web banner).

Use the approved masters (transparent SVG preferred, 2× PNG fallback), verbatim or flipped/rotated, rather than building fields by hand: Scattered Dots.

The rules for the dot field

Clearance is the rule most often broken. No copy, headline, button, card, logo, or image sits on top of a field, and nothing crops one. Leave at least one full grid pitch between the outermost dot and any other object (about 87px at 1920×1080, scaling with the pitch at other sizes). Cover no more than 50 percent of the space, keep copy in the clear area opposite the anchor, and use one field per layout, never two fields, and never a field with the big dot.

Building an on-brand variation

Prefer the masters. If a format outside the family is ever needed, these measured values define a valid field:

  • Grid: pitch = long edge ÷ 22; every dot identical, diameter 38.9% of the pitch, always on-grid (an 87.3px pitch with 34px dots at 1920×1080).
  • Density: 17–25% of grid cells filled, densest along the anchor edge and thinning outward.
  • One connected mass: no empty interior lines, no detached dots or clusters, no thin protruding arms; the longest straight run is 4 dots in any direction.
  • Flat fill only: perfect circles, no gradient, opacity, or stroke; max 3 orange, multicolor only.
Approval checklist

A field is on-brand only if every answer is yes: one clear centre of gravity; reads as a single connected field; a gradual dense-to-sparse transition; all dots the same size on one grid; an uneven but not chaotic outer contour; outliers visually connected to the mass; generous, intentional open space; stays abstract from a distance; no long lines, stair-steps, shapes, or repeated sequences; and, in multicolor, balanced colors with no secondary pattern.

The big dot

The big dot is the compact, high-impact form of the device: one dominant circle cropped by two edges, alone or with two to four smaller dots gathering and overlapping around it. The varied sizes are organizations of every scale, a large health system, a lab, a clinic, an app; the overlap is the point, separate systems no longer siloed but touching and exchanging, with Rhapsody as the anchoring dot the others connect around. The crop matters: a whole circle reads as a closed object, a bubble, pin, or logo mark; a cropped one reads as a network too large to fit in view, and forces a calm side for copy. Use it for display ads, social tiles, thin banners, cover slides, and hero and brand moments. (For big dot versus scattered field, see the comparison at the top of this card.)

A closed, approved family, use it, don’t build it

The masters are a closed, approved set across the backgrounds, placements, formats, and three variants, each as a transparent SVG and a 2× PNG. Pick the file that fits and use it as-is, flipped, or rotated 90°; never hand-build one or restyle it (no recoloring, resizing individual dots, gradients, opacity, or non-proportional stretching).

Built for the background

The anchor color and its companions are set by the background and are not interchangeable. The anchor is never the background hue, and never purple or orange (purple and orange are accents that stay on the companions).

Navy: Rhapsody blue anchor; teal, light blue, white companions.

Rhapsody blue: teal anchor; white, light blue, purple companions.

White: light blue anchor; Rhapsody blue, purple, navy companions.

Three variants

Solo, anchor only. For small or dense surfaces.

Default, anchor + 2–4 companions. The everyday choice.

Outlier, adds one orange dot: the next system, not yet connected.

What the parts mean

The device maps to the two-pillar story: the anchor is Rhapsody, the platform everything connects around; the larger companions are Build the infrastructure (the systems that connect, health systems, labs, clinics, apps); the smaller companions are Automate the work (the agents working across them); and the orange outlier is the next system, not yet connected. Use the outlier at most once, and omit it whenever the message claims completeness (a total, a finished state, “every” or “all” copy), or the graphic contradicts the line.

  • Anchor, cropped by two edges (about 25–45% off-frame) so it’s never a whole circle floating in the frame; navy, Rhapsody blue, teal, or light blue, never the background hue, purple, or orange.
  • Companions, 0 or 2–4, all different sizes. They may overlap the anchor but never each other; each stays a distinct, readable circle.
  • Flat fill, perfect circles, no gradient, opacity, or stroke.
  • Copy on the calm side, clear of the dots by at least one large-companion diameter; nothing sits on or crops the dots.
  • One device per layout, the big dot or a scattered field, never both. Formats: 16×9 (cover slides, web hero, video), 1×1 (social tile), 9×16 (story, vertical video), and a 728×90 thin banner. See Display ads and Email template for it in use.

Use the approved masters, verbatim or flipped/rotated, rather than hand-building: Brand devices.

Framing and diagram elements

Beyond backgrounds, the dots can be composed into shapes, a ring, an arc, a network, or a corner cluster, that frame content or carry a simple idea. Use them as decorative and structural accents in creative work: digital display ads, white papers and reports, banners, and slides. Here the dots are always equal size, always multicolor but never orange, and carry no gradient fill. A faint connecting line is allowed for a diagram, never a squiggle.

At a glance: do and don't

On-brand
  • Uniform scattered field (equal-size dots) on white, blue, or navy.
  • Single color or multicolor; multicolor includes up to three orange dots.
  • Anchored to a corner or edge, with a clear gap from text.
  • The one dot device in the layout.
Off-brand
  • Full-view takeover or more than 50 percent coverage.
  • Random placement, mixed sizes, or dots over text, faces, or the logo.
  • Off-palette, outlined, or gradient-filled dots.
  • Low-contrast pairings, or a solid block that never dissolves.

Dot row accent

A short horizontal row of dots is an approved compact use of the dot motif, useful in banners and email headers where a full dot field would be too much.

  • A few dots, one uniform size, evenly spaced.
  • Sequence through brand colors, for example teal, blue, light blue, purple.
  • Use as a small accent, not a full field. Do not combine with a dot field in the same layout.
