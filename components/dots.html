The dots pattern

The dot field is the signature Rhapsody graphic device: precise, structured, and rational. It says data moving between systems. Use one dot device per layout, anchored to a corner or edge with a clear gap from text, never behind headlines, faces, or the logo.

How the dots work

Dots are always structured, aligned to a grid. Either the size graduates (largest and densest at a corner, dissolving inward) or the field is uniform and scattered. Never random placement, and never a solid block: the field must dissolve, not read as bars.

Two patterns

Graduated field. The signature pattern: an aligned grid where dot size changes, largest and densest at a corner and shrinking smoothly inward as the field fades in the same direction. Single color or multicolor.

Graduated multicolor field on navy, anchored top-right.

Uniform scattered field. Equal-size dots on an aligned grid, dissolving from an edge. Single color or multicolor, and multicolor may include one to five orange dots.

Uniform scattered multicolor field on white, dissolving in from the right edge (a few orange accents).

The rules for both patterns

Anchor to a corner or edge, no more than 50 percent of the field, with a clear gap from text. On-palette colors only; the dots are exempt from the three-color rule and may include one to five orange dots. No outline-only dots, no gradient fills inside dots, and never dots plus another device in the same layout.

At a glance: do and don't

On-brand
  • Graduated field (size changes) or uniform scattered field (equal size), on white, blue, or navy.
  • Single color or multicolor; multicolor includes one to five orange dots. Exempt from the three-color rule.
  • Anchored to a corner or edge, no more than 50 percent, with a clear gap from text.
  • The one dot device in the layout.
Off-brand
  • Full-view takeover, or more than 50 percent with no open space.
  • Random placement or mixed sizes (reads as confetti).
  • Dots over headlines, faces, or the logo, or crowding text with no gap.
  • Off-palette colors, outline-only dots, or gradient fills inside dots.
  • Low-contrast pairings (such as blue dots on the blue field), or dots plus a squiggle in one layout.
  • Full columns with no gaps: the field must dissolve, never read as a solid block or bars.

Dot row accent

A short horizontal row of dots is an approved compact use of the dot motif, useful in banners and email headers where a full dot field would be too much.

  • A few dots, one uniform size, evenly spaced.
  • Sequence through brand colors, for example teal, blue, light blue, purple.
  • Use as a small accent, not a full field. Do not combine with a dot field in the same layout.
