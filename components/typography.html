Typography

Poppins is the primary typeface, with Arial as the fallback where Poppins is unavailable. Headings (H1 through H4 and the hero) set in Poppins Regular (400) for one consistent weight; body copy sets in Poppins Light (300). Medium (500) carries small emphasis such as labels and buttons, and Bold (700) is reserved for short standalone “moment” headlines (see Bold headlines for moments, below). IBM Plex Mono (weights 400 and 500) is the support typeface; see Eyebrows and Technical annotations below.

Bold headlines for moments

Headlines normally set in Poppins Regular (400). The exception is a short, standalone headline that marks a moment or a step in a journey rather than leading a block of narrative copy. With only a few words and no supporting paragraph to lend it weight, a Light or Regular headline reads thin, so set it in Poppins Bold (700) to give the moment the confidence it deserves. Reserve this for confirmation and milestone screens, a submitted form, a completed step, not for section or narrative headlines.

Do: bold for a standalone moment
You’re in!

Thanks for completing the survey. Watch your inbox for what’s next.

Don’t: light headline reads weak
You’re in!

A few words in Light float without a narrative around them.

  • Use Poppins Bold (700) only for short, standalone moment or confirmation headlines, for example a post-submission “You’re in!”
  • Keep narrative and section headlines in Regular (400); bold is the exception, not the default.
  • One bold moment headline per screen, paired with a short Regular supporting line.

Technical annotations (IBM Plex Mono)

Beyond eyebrows, IBM Plex Mono is the voice for small technical labeling: standards and data labels (FHIR, HL7, MCP, REST), technical captions under diagrams or code-adjacent visuals, and small technical UI accents such as ticker labels. Use weight 400 for running annotation text and 500 for emphasized labels, at caption sizes. It is a support face only: never body copy, headings, or buttons.

Implementation: load https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=IBM+Plex+Mono:wght@400;500&display=swap and reference it via the token --mono: "IBM Plex Mono", monospace;.

Dates, times, and name lockups

When a graphic carries a date, a time, or a person’s attribution, use these formats so event cards, webinar promos, and speaker graphics stay consistent.

Dates and times

  • Dates: abbreviated month, no period: Jan 4, 2026. Ranges use a hyphen with no spaces: Jan 1-4, 2026.
  • Times: lowercase a.m./p.m. with periods, hyphenated range, time zone after: 10-11 a.m. EST.
  • Multiple time zones separate with a pipe: 10-11 a.m. EST | 9-10 a.m. CST.

Name lockup

Attribute a person in three stacked lines (name, then title, then company) and keep the order fixed. Name in Poppins Medium (500), title in Poppins Regular (400) dark gray, company in Poppins Medium (500) Rhapsody Blue (light blue on dark grounds). Pair it with the thin-ring portrait treatment where a photo is present.

Two-tone headline

In a two-tone headline, set only the word or two you are emphasizing in the accent color. Every other word, and the supporting description below, stays white or black depending on the background. On light backgrounds the accent is Rhapsody Blue (#1A81F4) against navy or black text. On dark backgrounds it is also Rhapsody Blue at headline size, where it stays vibrant on navy; drop to light blue (#B4D8FF) only when the accent is small. The description line never takes the accent color.

Use it sparingly

The two-tone headline exists to pull the eye to genuinely meaningful text. Highlight one idea per headline, and don't reach for it on every headline: if everything is highlighted, nothing is. When in doubt, set the whole headline in a single color and save the accent for the moment that matters.

Health data, finally connected
On dark: at headline size the emphasis word is Rhapsody Blue; the rest of the headline and this description stay white.
Health data, finally connected
On light: the emphasis word is Rhapsody Blue; the rest and this description stay navy or black.
Do
  • Color only the one or two words carrying the emphasis.
  • Keep the rest of the headline, and the description, white (on dark) or navy/black (on light).
  • Reserve it for headlines where the highlighted idea genuinely matters.
Don’t
  • Color the whole headline, or the description line, in the accent.
  • Highlight several words, so the emphasis is diluted.
  • Apply it to every headline across a layout or deck.

Minimum font size (web)

For legibility on screen, keep body and interface text at a readable minimum. These are floors, not targets, most body copy should sit comfortably above them.

  • Body copy: at least 16px on web. This is the baseline for comfortable reading.
  • Secondary and supporting text: at least 14px.
  • Captions, labels, and fine print: at least 12px. Do not go below 12px for anything a user needs to read.
  • Legal or disclaimer text: at least 11px, used only where unavoidable.
  • Maintain at least 3:1 contrast for large text and 4.5:1 for normal body text, so size and contrast work together.
