Display ads

Display ads are small and seen fast, so they follow old billboard principles: a single message built for a glance. Lead with Rhapsody Blue or an approved photograph, and speak to a customer need in their own terms. The rules below give the specifics.

Leaderboard: a confident wordmark and one message, single CTA, and the big dot bled off the right edge, clear of the copy.

  • Rhapsody Blue first. Prefer a Rhapsody Blue background, it drives the best conversion from display. Navy and white are the alternates; a photography-led ad also performs well.
  • Billboard principle. Assume a glance. Keep text minimal and instantly readable; if it can’t be understood in a few seconds, cut it.
  • One message. These are small formats, so carry a single idea. Make clear what Rhapsody products do in terms of a customer need or challenge, or in the customer’s voice, not a feature list.
  • One CTA. A single call to action, aligned with the message. If the message is about speed, the CTA is “See how,” not a generic “Contact sales.”
  • One brand device. Carry one Rhapsody device so the ad is unmistakably ours, never two, offset and clear of the copy. The device depends on the background: on navy, a light glow (Aurora or Burst) or the dots; on Rhapsody Blue or white, the dots (light devices are navy-only). A photo can be the lead visual in place of a device.
  • Logo size. Make the wordmark a confident anchor, not an afterthought. It should be at least 25% of a square/vertical unit’s width, and at least 18% of a wide unit’s width (leaderboard/banner), never below 90px wide. Give it full clear space and keep it legible against its background.
  • Photography. Use approved library images (forward-facing, bright, modern workspace). Add a navy scrim so text and the wordmark stay legible and never fade into the photo, and keep text off busy areas. Follow the photography rules.
  • Type & color. White or navy text on blue; one accent word at most. For small blue elements on navy, drop text to light blue, but make a CTA a Rhapsody Blue pill (blue fill, white label) rather than small blue text.
  • Sizing. Design one layout that adapts across standard units (300×250, 300×600, 160×600, 728×90); keep the message and wordmark inside a safe margin.
Do
  • Lead with a Rhapsody Blue background or an approved photo.
  • One compelling, customer-focused message understood at a glance.
  • One CTA that matches the message.
  • Minimal text; wordmark small and clear.
Don’t
  • Pack in multiple messages, feature lists, or long copy.
  • Add two competing CTAs or a generic mismatched one.
  • Set text over a busy part of a photo with no scrim.
  • Reach for tertiary accent colors or tiny body copy.
