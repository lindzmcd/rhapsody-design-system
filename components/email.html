Email

Email uses the same system as the web: a navy header band carrying the wordmark and an Aurora glow, a teal seam where the header meets the body, then navy or black copy on white for maximum legibility. Keep one message and one primary action per send.

  • Header. Navy band with the white wordmark and an optional Aurora glow offset to one side. Add the program or series name (for example, “The Modern Integrator”) in light blue beneath the wordmark. Close the band with the teal seam.
  • Body. Black or navy body copy on white, Poppins, generous line spacing. Glows and dot fields stay in the header, not behind body text.
  • One CTA. A single primary button, teal on the light body. Secondary links can be inline text in Rhapsody Blue.
  • Imagery. Follow the photography rules; portraits use the thin-ring treatment. Keep the clear margin around any graphic.
  • Accessibility. Maintain the 3:1 minimum contrast; don't set long copy in light blue or teal on white.

Employee email signature

A consistent signature keeps everyday email on brand. Keep it text-based and simple so it renders in every client: the wordmark, a thin teal divider, then the name lockup and contact lines. Use Poppins where the client supports it, with Arial as the fallback. Set copy in navy, dark gray, and Rhapsody Blue on a white background; don't add background colors, images behind text, or quotes.

  • Wordmark. Use the primary logo (navy bar, blue wordmark) at roughly 120–140px wide on the white signature. Never stretch, recolor, or drop a glow behind it. In tight signatures the R monogram may stand in.
  • Divider. One thin teal rule about the cap height of the wordmark separates the logo from the text, the same device as the co-brand lockup. No box around either element.
  • Name lockup. Name in Poppins Medium (500) navy, then title in Regular (400) dark gray. Don’t repeat the company name in the text; the wordmark lockup to the left already carries it.
  • Contact lines. Phone and email in dark gray; the website as an actionlink ending in a trailing arrow (rhapsody.health →) in Rhapsody Blue. Keep everything at least 12px.
  • Keep it clean. No quotes, taglines set as images, banners, or social icons in unapproved colors. If a campaign banner is required, use an approved template rather than building one inline.
  • Certifications (required roles). Some roles must display compliance badges (for example Cyber Essentials Plus or ISO 27001). Add them as a small row about 44px tall below the signature, using the official issued badge art only, hosted like the logo. Keep even spacing, and never stretch, crop, or recolor a certification mark.
  • Company name. Always “Rhapsody,” never “Rhapsody Health.”
