Big dot

The dots are Rhapsody’s primary graphic device, and they carry the meaning of what we do: each dot is a data point, a system, a person, and Rhapsody connects, cleans, and orders them. Two compositions tell that story, the fluid dot pattern (its own card) for large canvases, and the big dot for compact, high-impact moments. The big dot is one dominant circle cropped by two edges, the anchor, with two to four smaller companions gathering and overlapping around it. The varied sizes are organizations of every scale; the overlap is the point, separate systems touching and exchanging, with Rhapsody as the anchoring dot the others connect around.

A closed, approved family, use it, don’t build it

The big dots are a closed, approved ecosystem, one set per background with three crops each, shipped as transparent SVG and 1×/2× PNG and JPG. Use one as-is, flipped, or rotated 90°; never hand-build, recolor, or restyle it.

Fluid pattern or big dot?

Use one dot device per layout, never both, and never a dot device with a light device. Reach for the fluid dot pattern on larger canvases with room to breathe, full or section backgrounds, web heroes, slide and report covers, where the pattern can dissolve gradually from an edge. Reach for the big dot on small, fast-read formats, display ads, social tiles, thin banners, cover slides, plus hero and brand moments that want warmth. The decorative dot accents are a separate, lighter use.

What the parts mean

The parts map to the two-pillar story: the anchor is Rhapsody; larger companions are the systems it connects (Build the infrastructure); smaller companions are the agents across them (Automate the work). An optional single orange outlier can mark the next system, not yet connected, use it at most once and omit it when the message claims completeness (“every,” “all,” a finished total).

Backgrounds and dot colors

Each set is built for one background and is not interchangeable. The anchor is never the background hue; purple and orange stay on the companions, never the anchor.

The fluid dot pattern keeps its own registers (white on navy, white on blue, dots on white, multicolor on navy), detailed on its card. Orange lives in the fluid multicolor field only, never on a blue ground, and never more than 3 dots so it reads as a surprise. The dots are exempt from the three-color rule.

  • Anchor, cropped by two edges (about 25–45% off-frame) so it’s never a whole circle floating in the frame.
  • Companions, 2–4, all different sizes. They may overlap the anchor but never each other; each stays a distinct, readable circle.
  • Flat fill, perfect circles, no gradient, opacity, or stroke.
  • Copy on the calm side, clear of the dots by at least one large-companion diameter; nothing sits on or crops the dots.
  • One device per layout, big dot or fluid pattern, never both, and never with a light device. Cover no more than 50% of the space. Formats: 16×9 (cover slides, web hero, video), 1×1 (social tile), 9×16 (story, vertical video), and a 728×90 thin banner.
