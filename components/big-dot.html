Big dot

The compact, high-impact form of the dot device (see Dots overview for when to use the big dot versus the scattered field). It is one dominant circle cropped by two edges, alone or with two to four smaller dots gathering and overlapping around it. The varied sizes are organizations of every scale, a large health system, a lab, a clinic, an app; the overlap is the point, separate systems no longer siloed but touching and exchanging, with Rhapsody as the anchoring dot the others connect around. The crop matters: a whole circle reads as a closed object, a bubble, pin, or logo mark; a cropped one reads as a network too large to fit in view, and forces a calm side for copy. Use it for display ads, social tiles, thin banners, cover slides, and hero and brand moments.

A closed, approved family, use it, don’t build it

Like the field, the big dots are a closed, approved set—one for each background, placement, format, and the three variants, as transparent SVG and 2× PNG. Use one as-is, flipped, or rotated 90°; never hand-build or restyle it.

The anchor color and its companions are set by the background and are not interchangeable. The anchor is never the background hue, and never purple or orange (purple and orange are accents that stay on the companions): on navy, a Rhapsody blue anchor with teal, light blue, and white companions; on Rhapsody blue, a teal anchor with white, light blue, and purple; on white, a light blue anchor with Rhapsody blue, purple, and navy.

Three ways to use it

Solo, anchor only. For small or dense surfaces.

Default, anchor + 2–4 companions.

Outlier, adds one orange dot: the next system, not yet connected.

What the parts mean

The parts map to the two-pillar story: the anchor is Rhapsody; larger companions are the systems it connects (Build the infrastructure); smaller companions are the agents across them (Automate the work); the orange outlier is the next system, not yet connected. Use the outlier at most once, and omit it when the message claims completeness (“every,” “all,” a finished total).

  • Anchor, cropped by two edges (about 25–45% off-frame) so it’s never a whole circle floating in the frame. Colors follow the background (above).
  • Companions, 0 or 2–4, all different sizes. They may overlap the anchor but never each other; each stays a distinct, readable circle.
  • Flat fill, perfect circles, no gradient, opacity, or stroke.
  • Copy on the calm side, clear of the dots by at least one large-companion diameter; nothing sits on or crops the dots.
  • One device per layout, the big dot or a scattered field, never both. Formats: 16×9 (cover slides, web hero, video), 1×1 (social tile), 9×16 (story, vertical video), and a 728×90 thin banner. See Display ads and Email template for it in use.

Masters (SVG + 2× PNG), used verbatim or flipped/rotated: Brand devices.
