Layout and spacing

Layouts should always breathe. A few simple rules keep graphics and documents clean and consistent.

Margins and padding

Margins are the empty safe zone at the outer edge of a composition or page. Padding is the breathing room around and between the objects inside it, graphics, charts, images, text boxes, and callouts. They are set separately, and the right amount differs by application.

Outer margin (the safe zone)

For a self-contained graphic, a hero, ad, or social post, keep a clear margin of about 6 to 7 percent of the shorter side, with no copy, photo, or logo inside it (roughly a 40px margin on a 600px-wide graphic, scaling proportionally). That figure is correct for single graphics; multi-page documents follow their application’s page-margin convention instead.

  • Web: use 6–7% for self-contained graphics. Responsive page containers use a consistent gutter of about 24–40px on desktop and 16–20px on mobile.
  • PowerPoint (16:9): keep all content inside a safe margin of about 0.4–0.5in from every slide edge (roughly 5–6% of the 7.5in height). Align titles and footers to that same margin.
  • Word and print documents: use standard page margins of about 1in (never below 0.75in). Document margins are intentionally wider than the 6–7% graphic rule; don’t shrink them to match it.

Object padding (space around and between objects)

Every object, a chart, image, table, text box, or callout, keeps clear space around it so nothing touches or crowds. Hold a consistent minimum:

  • Web: at least 16–24px between objects and between an object and nearby text; container/card inner padding at least 16–24px.
  • PowerPoint: at least 0.2–0.3in between objects and between an object and its title or caption.
  • Word: at least 0.1–0.15in of wrap spacing around figures and tables, with a blank line above and below.
  • Never let a graphic, dot field, or glow touch or run under text; keep that gap at least the outer margin.

Rule of thumb: the 6–7% figure is the outer safe margin for a single graphic. Padding between objects is a separate, smaller gutter that keeps a busy layout legible. When in doubt, add space.

Separator line (the seam)

When a graphic borders other content, such as a webpage hero or an email banner seam, a thin rule sits along one edge (the bottom by default) to separate it from the content below. On standalone pieces like ads or social posts, no line is needed. Keep it thin and full-width. Teal is the default and signature seam where a navy or dark band meets the body; light blue is an approved alternative on navy. On a white or light band, use Rhapsody Blue (preferred for digital) or navy for the seam.

Module accent line (H2 sections)

Section modules led by an H2 open with a short horizontal accent line (“accent bar”) set directly above the heading, or above the eyebrow where one is present. It signals the start of a new content module and gives each section a consistent, engineered entry point. Adopted from the homepage prototype.

Teal bar: automation / “automate the work” contexts.

  • Dimensions: 44px wide × 3px tall, 2px corner radius.
  • Spacing: sits above the eyebrow/H2 with about 20–22px below it before the heading.
  • Color carries the section’s pathway: blue (#1A81F4) for Build, teal (#23C5BF) for Automate; default to blue when neutral. This is a brand-wide wayfinding system, see Pathways: build and automate for the full directive and the color order when more than two items appear. Apply the color to the line only; keep the eyebrow and heading navy or blue, since teal is not legible as small text on white.
  • One accent line per module, tied to the primary H2. Do not repeat it on sub-cards within the same module (cards use their own thin top bar where needed).
  • It is decorative structure, not a divider; don’t stretch it full-width.
  • The same device appears on section headers, pillar cards, and case cards, always at the same 44×3 size, so the whole site reads as one system.
  • Hold one accent color across a whole activation, a deck, landing page, email, or report, and change it only when the context itself changes (for example a dedicated automation section), not surface to surface.
