Color

Rhapsody Blue is the primary color, used most prominently. Rhapsody Navy, light blue, and teal are secondary. Purple and orange are tertiary accents, used sparingly. Full-page backgrounds stay white, Rhapsody Blue, or Rhapsody Navy; light gray and gray-blue are for content modules, not full-page fills.

Text color

Body copy uses only white, Rhapsody Navy, or black. Rhapsody Blue is an accent color, never running body copy. Work down the list in order.

  • Rhapsody Navy is the default text color. On light backgrounds, set body copy and headings in Rhapsody Navy (#0B2C47) rather than black. Navy is the on-brand dark and clears contrast on white easily.
  • Black is a fallback, not a default. Reach for black (#050F19) only when navy falls short of a contrast or accessibility requirement for a given pairing, or when print is limited to options that can’t reproduce navy accurately (for example single-color or limited-ink jobs). Otherwise, navy.
  • Rhapsody Blue is the accent on light. Use it for emphasis, links, and one accent word in a headline, not for running body copy.
  • On dark backgrounds, text is white. For blue accents on navy, the two blues split by size—see “Rhapsody Blue on navy” below.
  • No tertiary color in type: teal, purple, and orange are never used for body copy, headings, eyebrows, or labels. Two exceptions: text links in automation contexts may use teal (#23C5BF; see Text links and the CTA arrow in Buttons and CTAs), and an H3 may take a secondary color (navy, light blue, or teal, contrast permitting). H1, H2, body copy, and captions stay within the copy colors above.
  • Color spaces: digital uses HEX or RGB; print uses CMYK or Pantone.
The three-color rule, and its exceptions

No more than three brand colors per layout, primary and secondary included. Two exceptions: the multicolor dot field (its own system, full palette) and data visualizations, where charts may add colors to distinguish data (see Charts, graphs, and tables). The rest of the layout still holds to three.

Color accessibility

Type and background combinations must meet a minimum 3:1 contrast ratio. Checking contrast is always your responsibility. A good rule of thumb: navy or white type plus one other color. The swatches below show contrast physics only, which pairings clear the threshold; brand usage still governs (for example, tertiary colors are never used for type, see Text color).

Text on teal

Teal (#23C5BF) is a light color, so a teal fill is a background for dark text only—navy or black. White on teal is 2.1:1 and fails at every size, so a larger font can’t rescue it; if a layout needs light text, darken the surface to navy.

Minimum sizes for copy on teal

  • Set all copy on teal in navy or black. Body and interface text at least 16px; supporting labels at least 14px; never below 12px.
  • Same rule for teal buttons: the label is navy (as in the dark-mode primary button), never white.

Rhapsody Blue on navy

On navy (#0B2C47), the two blues split by size. Rhapsody Blue (#1A81F4) reads at 3.75:1, which clears 3:1 for large or heavy type but falls short of the 4.5:1 needed for small body text. Light blue (#B4D8FF) reads at about 9.7:1 and stays legible on navy at any size. So use Rhapsody Blue for large, vibrant moments and light blue for anything small. Light blue is the small-scale counterpart to blue on dark grounds, not a wholesale replacement for it.

When to use which on navy

  • Rhapsody Blue on navy: large headlines and accent words (roughly 24px+, or ~19px bold), plus sizable graphic elements such as large bars, blocks, and big dots, where the blue stays vibrant.
  • Light blue on navy: body copy, captions, labels, secondary and accent lines, thin rules, small icons, and small dots, anything below the large-text threshold.
  • Rule of thumb: if the element is large enough to pass 3:1, Rhapsody Blue is fine; if it is small text that needs 4.5:1, switch to light blue.
  • Don’t rely on size alone to rescue small Rhapsody Blue copy on navy, it stays below 4.5:1; drop to light blue instead.
