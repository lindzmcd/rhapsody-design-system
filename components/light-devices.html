Light devices

Alongside the dots, three soft light-based devices carry atmosphere. Where the dots are precise and rational, these are warm and human, together they say engineered, but human. Use them for photo-free digital moments (heroes, social, slides) on navy or dark backgrounds only, never behind photography.

Rules for the light devices

Light devices sit on navy or dark grounds only. On a Rhapsody Blue or white background, don’t use a glow, use the dots device or photography instead. Glows use teal, blue, and purple only, never orange (orange lives in the dots and photo-frame rings). Keep each glow within three colors. Offset the glow into open space so copy sits on the calm side of the composition, never on the brightest point.

The light devices

Aurora: a soft blended wash. The calm we bring. Teal-led, fading to blue.

Aurora ribbon: a flowing band of light, a second register of Aurora, for motion and large applications (see rule). Teal through blue to a touch of purple.

Burst: soft rays radiating from a core and fading out. A quiet, atmospheric backdrop for breakthrough moments. Purple-led at the core, fading to blue.

Plexus: a connected network of points, for connectivity and intelligence moments (see rule). Blue-led with teal nodes.

Which glow color

Each device carries a default color drawn from the meaning it holds, all within the teal / blue / purple glow palette (never orange, and each glow within three colors). Aurora is teal-led, fading to blue, the calm we bring. Plexus is blue-led with teal nodes, the connected, reliable platform. Burst is purple-led at the core, fading to blue, for breakthrough and launch moments. The Aurora ribbon runs teal through blue to a touch of purple, the one place all three flow together, for AI and motion. By context: reliability and infrastructure moments lean blue, calm and automation moments lean teal, and breakthrough or AI-forward moments bring in purple as the accent, echoing the Build (blue) and Automate (teal) pathways.

When to use Plexus

Plexus is the connected-network device, the most literal expression of interoperability and intelligence. Reserve it for moments where connection or the AI / agentic platform is the point: behind testimonials and customer quotes, and on platform or Rhapsody Axon sections. It is not a general-purpose glow; use Aurora or Burst for atmosphere. As with every device, one per layout, offset from the copy, on navy or dark grounds.

When to use the Aurora ribbon

The Aurora ribbon, a flowing band of light, is for motion and large-scale applications, video, animated headers, and large hero or event backdrops, where it has room to move and is not hugging copy. It is a great way to suggest our AI capabilities, modern, fluid, and seamless, without being literal. For everyday layouts, banners, and anywhere text sits close, use the regular Aurora wash instead.

Devices in use

Produced examples across display formats, showing each device in context: Aurora as a calm wash and Burst for a launch moment. Copy sits on the calm side, the light-blue second line is a consistent device, and the dots stay a separate accent rather than mixed into a glow. One device leads per layout, never two.

  • Teal, blue, purple only. No orange in glows.
  • Offset the glow; keep copy on the calm side, not the bright center.
  • Digital hero, social, and slide atmosphere. Not behind photography, and not a universal print element.
  • Maintain a clear margin of about 6 to 7 percent of the shorter side around the composition.
  • Use one graphic device, dots or a light device, per layout, not both at once.

Do not use in new applications

Retired for new work
  • The squiggle line. It appears in the brand guide, but we are not using it in any new applications of the brand.
  • The graduated dot field. The size-graduated grid is retired. In practice we apply the uniform scattered field (equal-size dots), so use that for every new dot background.
  • Icon-on-circle product illustrations. The bold dimensional illustrations that layer an icon over a filled blue circle are not to be used in new applications. Use the product logo, or the line icons below, instead.

If you inherit an older asset that uses either, plan to phase it out rather than extend it.
