Quick corrections log

A record of what is retired and what changed. The section cards are the current rules; where an entry here conflicts with a card, the card wins.

✕ RETIRED

Do not use

Retired for all new work. No exceptions.

  • Busy circle / photo / icon-overlay montages — the composite hero of headshots orbiting an illustration with floating speech bubbles and shapes. Use real photography, the fluid dot pattern, or an approved Aurora master.
  • Legacy line-art / spot illustrations and character-driven cartoon scenes — including outlined “person + cloud + lock” artwork and whimsical people. Replace with photography, product imagery, diagrams, data visualization, or approved devices.
  • Two-tone / two-color icons — including icon-on-circle art. Replaced by the single outline family (stroke-only, 1.7 weight, currentColor).
  • Legacy thick single-color rings around headshots — the teal, orange, or purple rings from the earlier brand guide. Portraits use the treatments in Photography → Portrait treatments: the navy-and-blue brand ring, the thin ring, the arc accent, the card, or the editorial split.
  • Squiggle line with dots — the wandering hairline path with colored nodes. Use the fluid dot pattern or Aurora.
  • Burst and Plexus light devices — the light device is Aurora only.
  • Scattered / structured dot field (the earlier 45-composition grid family, df-* art) — replaced by the Fluid dot pattern.
  • Loose dot scatters and constellations anywhere except the homepage hero (see Web pages).
  • Teal-filled buttons and darkened teals — the primary pill is Rhapsody Blue, and #23C5BF is the only teal.

October 2026

  • Guide trimmed to the core system. The display-ad, email-template, hero, social-post, and product-launch example cards were removed; the rules they used live in the foundation, component, and device cards.
  • Brand expression gained a decision hierarchy, naming rule, and pre-ship checklist.
  • Fluid dot pattern replaced the scattered dot field (same four registers, new verbatim masters). Big dot became a closed ecosystem: one set per ground, three crops each.
  • Aurora is the only light device. Burst and Plexus retired; the wash and the ribbon merged into one device that allows movement.
  • Hero badge graphics (verbatim website art) and Web pages (the refreshed homepage and solutions pages) added.
  • Teal is immutable, and teal type lives on navy only; the teal tick carries pathway color on light grounds. Teal-filled pill retired.
  • Rhapsody Blue on navy is allowed for display type 24px and up (two-tone highlight, stat numerals); light blue for smaller text. Two-tone headlines run on navy or white grounds only.
  • Color gained Color in composition, a single color order shared by charts and pathways, and functional status colors. Sanctioned gradients are Aurora, the web navy hero gradient, and photo scrims.
  • Portraits: the brand ring (website-hero weight) is the default; the thin ring is for small avatars. Caption size raised to 12px to match the minimum-size floor.

September 2026

  • Brand expression and Illustration added; character-driven illustration retired.
  • Color: purple and orange restricted to accents, emphasis, and data visualization; accessibility thresholds and the approved color-pair matrix added; text on teal removed.
  • Typography: leading and tracking simplified; edit the copy rather than tightening type to fit.
  • Devices at a glance, Icon library (tile colors), and Photography (technology-first imagery) tightened.

July 2026

  • Logo: primary bar locked to Rhapsody Navy; blue-wordmark / white-line lockup added for large sizes on dark.
  • Light blue refreshed to #B4D8FF, replacing #90C5FF.
  • Typography: one-weight hierarchy (Regular), with Medium for the hero, H1, and moment headlines; eyebrows moved to IBM Plex Mono; date, time, and name-lockup formats added.
  • Components: single outline icon family, module accent line, CTA arrow on text links, bracket logo treatment, dot row accent, seam, and employee email signature added.
  • Multicolor dots confirmed on-brand (an earlier draft labeled them off-brand).
