Graphic devices at a glance

Rhapsody works from one connected family of visual devices. Each carries a distinct meaning and a recommended color lead, so the device reinforces the message rather than just decorating it.

The device system is intentionally limited. Fewer choices create a more recognizable, premium brand and make AI-generated work more consistent. Usually lead with one visual expression per layout, but the evolved devices can be combined with restraint when it genuinely helps (see below).

Device Meaning Why / when to use
Aurora When everything comes together, harmony across the system. Allows movement. The one light device, with range from a soft wash to a flowing form. Use for harmony, ease, and connected systems, and for motion, video, animated headers, and large backdrops. Navy or dark backgrounds only.
Fluid dot pattern Scale and order, data resolving into one connected field. Use to communicate data, infrastructure, scale, and systems working together. A half-tone dot grid that flows dense-to-sparse; best for larger compositions where the graphic can occupy its own visual space. Can also carry a two-tone color-territory split.
Big dot Relationship and momentum, systems of every size connecting. Use for simple, high-impact brand moments where a full graphic field would be too complex. Best for small or fast-read formats, hero moments, and compositions that need a strong visual anchor.
Photography The human industry we serve, people first. Use when people, expertise, collaboration, or real-world technology environments strengthen the story. Photography should make Rhapsody feel credible, modern, and human while balancing the more abstract graphic devices.
Master-art rule

Do not recreate or alter graphic devices. Use the approved SVG/PNG master art as provided.

Combine with restraint

Lead with one device by default, but the evolved devices can work together when it makes sense: a big dot alongside a photo, or the fluid dot pattern as a quiet background behind a photo, both pair well. Don’t overdo it, two complementary elements at most, never a stack of devices competing for attention, and nothing placed over faces or copy. If a layout feels empty, prefer open space over adding decoration.
