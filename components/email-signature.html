Email signature

A consistent employee signature keeps everyday email on brand. Keep it text-based and simple so it renders in every client: the wordmark, a thin teal divider, then the name lockup and contact lines. Use Poppins where the client supports it, with Arial as the fallback. Set copy in navy, dark gray, and Rhapsody Blue on a white background; don’t add background colors, images behind text, or quotes.

  • Wordmark. Use the primary logo (navy bar, blue wordmark) at roughly 120–140px wide on the white signature. Never stretch, recolor, or drop a glow behind it. In tight signatures the R monogram may stand in.
  • Divider. One thin teal rule about the cap height of the wordmark separates the logo from the text, the same device as the co-brand lockup. No box around either element.
  • Name lockup. Name in Poppins Medium (500) navy, then title in Regular (400) dark gray. Don’t repeat the company name in the text; the wordmark lockup to the left already carries it.
  • Contact lines. Phone and email in dark gray; the website as an actionlink ending in a trailing arrow (rhapsody.health →) in Rhapsody Blue. Keep everything at least 12px.
  • Keep it clean. No quotes, taglines set as images, banners, or social icons in unapproved colors. If a campaign banner is required, use an approved template rather than building one inline.
  • Certifications (required roles). Some roles must display compliance badges (for example Cyber Essentials Plus or ISO 27001) — see below.
  • Company name. Always “Rhapsody,” never “Rhapsody Health.”

With required certifications

Where a role must show compliance badges, add them in a row below the signature, separated by a thin divider. Both current badges are stacked (vertical) lockups, so size them to a consistent height of about 44px with even spacing. The frames below show placement and size; drop in the official issued badge art (transparent background) in place of each frame. Host it like the logo, and never stretch, crop, or recolor a certification mark.
